Capturing the intent to play when the action is blocked.

Overview

Chess.com has 200M members, and the person I most want to play is usually mid-game.

I have 24 friends on Chess.com. Playing a stranger is fine. Playing someone I know is better - I have history, I know their openings, there's something at stake beyond the rating.

But almost every time I open the app, my friends are either offline or already in a game. The moment I want to play them is the one moment I can't.

The problem

I can't challenge a friend who is playing.

Chess.com players open the app and casually play random opponents. That's the default behavior - mine and my friends'. Which means if a friend is online at all, they're usually mid-game.

So I tried the second route: messaging.

The message button is hard to find, which I understand. Chess.com is a chess product before it's a social product; the playing experience should dominate. What I didn't expect was this:

My friend never got a notification.

My message went into the void. He finished his game and immediately started another one, with no idea I was waiting.

My workaround

I found a detour to get him noticed.

I found his live game as a spectator, watched until the position was resolved, and sat with my finger over the challenge button waiting for that two-second window.

Eventually I was able to challenge him, but this took too much time and effort from me. Knowing that the next time I want to casually play him will be like this is even more insane.

The obvious objection

Why not just message him somewhere else?

Messenger, Discord, anywhere. Because I met him on Chess.com. That's how most chess friendships start - I play someone good, I add them, and the friendship lives entirely inside the product.

Which is the actual point. If the intent to play has to leave Chess.com to get coordinated, Chess.com is leaking the game type it should want most.

What I tried first

v1 - Message notifications

The smallest possible fix: notify people when they get a message, the way League of Legends and FC Online do. Keep it small enough not to break focus.

The obvious objection is disruption - any noise during a blitz or bullet game is a cost. That's solvable: hold the notification until the game ends.

Killed it. A delayed notification still hands both people work at the worst possible moment. He has to read it, decide, navigate back to me, and send a challenge - all in the post-game window where attention has already left the building. It defers the information.

The queue defers the intent. Nobody has to coordinate at all.

If you explored other directions - auto-rematch, notify-me-when-free, a general "looking for a game" status - add them here with a one-line kill each. Two dead ends is good; three is better.

v2 - [ name the design ]

The Challenge button isn't failing because I lack permission. It's failing on timing. Those are different failures and they deserve different responses - a permission failure should say no, a timing failure should say not yet.

So instead of returning an error state at peak motivation, capture the intent and execute it at the earliest valid moment.

The psychology is the part I find interesting. The intent to play a specific person is perishable - it peaks the instant I see their name and decays fast. A blocked action doesn't pause that decay, it accelerates it, because now I've been told no. Queuing converts a dead end into an open loop: I've committed, he can see it, and the commitment does the waiting instead of me.

Reflection

What I'd take from this.

Two items, three lines each, in your own voice. Candidates, if useful:

  • The workaround was the research. Nothing in the interviews-and-personas playbook would have found the two-second gap. Spectating my friend's game to time a challenge told me more than a study would have.
  • The first fix and the real fix aren't the same size. Message notifications were the obvious answer and would have shipped faster. They also would have left the actual problem - coordination at the worst possible moment - completely intact.
